# Spoken narration

Write for listening: clear subjects/actions, purposeful links, varied sentence lengths and ordinary words. Keep required meaning and source facts; do not add claims to make prose appear vivid. Avoid generic praise, formulaic hook/CTA, repeated explanation and boilerplate lists.

The channel is explanatory English, not mandatory comic banter. A1-language material is only appropriate when the brief selects it. No arbitrary 256-character creative ceiling: synthesis chunking is a backend concern and the measured WAV determines timing.

Keep narration/visible_text/captions/coverage/anchors spelling exact, including I. Pronunciation substitutions stay in synthesis input. Freeze narration before quotes/anchors; editing later creates a new content version and impact plan.

Language, aspect and subtitles are independent contract fields. Rate is a chosen synthesis setting, not proof of intelligibility. Do not infer pronunciation/acting from ASR or a prompt.
