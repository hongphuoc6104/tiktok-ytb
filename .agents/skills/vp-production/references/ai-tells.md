# Editing narration for natural speech

Use this reference only when phrasing sounds mechanical. These are editing cues, not evidence that a person used AI and not a checklist that every sentence must satisfy.

- Replace unnecessary formal words with familiar ones when meaning stays exact.
- Remove praise, vague significance and filler that do not help the learner follow the situation.
- Prefer concrete subjects/actions over unspecified experts, observers or passive attribution. Verify uncertain factual claims against the recorded source; do not invent one.
- Vary sentence length and transitions as the action develops. Repeated three-part lists or identical openings can flatten narration, but a useful parallel construction is allowed.
- Keep precise vocabulary, correct grammar, quotes and the one-sense contract. Do not simplify by dropping required meaning or changing approved data.
- Remove chatbot replies, unfinished placeholders, internal IDs and citation/control tokens such as `oai_citation`, `:contentReference` or unresolved span markers from spoken/visible text. Preserve legitimate URLs/attribution in source metadata.
- Read narration as speech before freezing anchors. After submission, use versioned repair; do not edit quote/coverage independently to disguise a changed line.

Formatting choices, formal tone or correct grammar alone are not problems. The target is clear, connected English serving the story and learning goal. Check questionable sources through project references or primary documentation within the task scope; missing evidence is reported precisely and does not become a fabricated citation or an automatic request for approval.
