# Vocabulary pedagogy for Video Pilot

Use for vocabulary lessons only. The current bank-generated brief and selected sense are authoritative; do not teach sibling senses as extra targets.

## Learning contract

Define one observable outcome: recognize the intended action/meaning and use the word in a short appropriate sentence. Separate this from view counts, completion and comments. Use the learner's level to choose vocabulary load, sentence length and response effort.

New lessons may combine these functions instead of allocating one scene to each:
- A concrete situation or question makes the word useful immediately.
- An action/image plus plain explanation establishes the selected meaning.
- Connected examples demonstrate usage and a meaningful change or contrast.
- A model utterance gives the learner something accurate to say.
- A retrieval turn with feedback and a natural next action checks learning.

There is no universal requirement for five pedagogical parts, three unrelated situations, etymology, IPA, linking, two repeats or a fixed number of images. Respect an existing brief that explicitly requires any of these; revise the brief through the workflow if the requirements conflict. New channel defaults express learning functions, not a mandatory spoken template.

## Story and examples

Build a causal micro-story: goal → obstacle → action → consequence. Preserve the protagonist's situation across examples or make a contrast serve the same question. Changing Sunday to Monday and then introducing a running friend is not automatically a resolution.

Start with a recognizable inner thought, action, contradiction or answerable question. Reveal enough context for the learner to understand the promise; deliver it before requesting engagement. Do not reuse the same alarm/bed introduction for every related word. Avoid shame, generic greetings, generic praise and forced cliffhangers.

Teach usage with correct English sentences and the selected meaning. Compare a confusable word only when useful to this learner; do not force a false contrast or invent a word origin. A mnemonic is a memory aid, not a complete dictionary definition. Verify pronunciation/meaning claims from reliable references when needed.

## Practice that exists in the artifact

Choose the activity first: listen-and-repeat, shadowing, completion, choice or sentence production. Explain it in learner language. A static mascot is not a moving mouth demonstration. Avoid vague “chuẩn Tây” claims; focus on the intended sound, stress and intelligibility.

For listen-and-repeat or a question, reserve a real response interval. In content-v3, `scenes[].audio_direction.vi/en.learner_pause_seconds` requests a quiet hold at that scene's end; put the model/question immediately before it. Set the interval for the actual utterance and verify on WAV. The total duration includes this time. Do not insert bracketed production instructions into narration.

Give an answer or feedback after a retrieval turn. A supported sentence stem can lower the barrier to an A1 comment; a generic request to comment is not an assessment. Practice and CTA may share one action if both functions remain clear.

## Visual and listening load

Choose enough images to show changes in meaning or state. Do not cut during the learner's turn merely to meet an image quota. Display target words near their referents; keep teaching text clear of captions and platform controls. New terms, dense text and fast motion compete for attention.

Review in the learner's language and cultural context. Check the English itself, image-to-meaning correspondence and actual response time. A valid coverage quote, a high image count or two printed repetitions cannot establish educational quality.
